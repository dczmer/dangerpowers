# Layer 2 -- Rhetorical / Structural Patterns (technical-writing)

Paraphrased from the source corpus. Illustrations are synthetic.

1. **TLDR first, detail after.** Open a topic or section with a `TLDR:` label and a one- or two-sentence punchline, then drill down, so a reader can stop early. Illustration: "TLDR: keep the parsing separate from the business logic. Everything below is why."
2. **Numbered or checkbox decomposition for anything actionable.** Plans, status trackers, and reading lists become nested numbered or `- [ ]` lists, not prose. Sub-steps carry inline notes such as "NOTE: this is where i'm stuck."
3. **"How might we..." as the statement of intent.** Close a roadmap or planning section with one italicized HMW question that turns a wall of bullets into a single testable goal.
4. **Parenthetical self-annotation mid-sentence.** Interrupt a sentence to flag a caveat, a reviewer question, or a personal aside, sometimes tagged, e.g. `(CQ)` for a content question. Illustration: "(CQ): this tip probably belongs in its own section somewhere."
5. **TODOs left in place.** Leave `TODO:` markers in the published text as first-class content: open questions, unverified jobs, missing sections. Do not resolve or hide them.
6. **Challenges named before the plan.** Before the solution, list the known difficulties under a plain lead-in such as "The challenges we will face are:".
7. **Rapid pass/fail survey of options.** When comparing candidate solutions, give each one a quick verdict marker (`:x:` / `:white_check_mark:`) and a one- or two-line reason, not comparative paragraphs.
8. **Before/after code pairs.** Show a refactor as the literal "before" snippet followed by the literal "after" snippet, with little prose between them.
9. **Owner-tagged action items.** In meeting-notes-style docs, put the owner's name in parentheses or caps at the front of a numbered item instead of using a separate table.
10. **Doubt left visible.** Voice uncertainty mid-explanation ("I'm not sure...", "maybe...", "TODO: does this...") instead of smoothing it into a confident statement.
